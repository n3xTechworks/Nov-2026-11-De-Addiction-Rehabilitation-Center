Am I ready to go back?

Going back to work is a big, positive step. A job brings structure, purpose and confidence. It can also bring stress, long hours and situations where alcohol is present. Before you return, talk it through with your counsellor. Most people feel ready when they are sleeping reasonably well, have a relapse-prevention plan and have support lined up for evenings and weekends.

What should I tell my employer?

You do not have to share your diagnosis. A doctor's certificate can simply say you were receiving medical treatment. Some people choose to tell their manager or HR more, especially if they need adjustments such as time off for aftercare sessions. If you are unsure, a simple script helps: “I took time off to look after my health. I am doing well and I have a plan to stay well.”

In India, medical information shared with an employer should be kept confidential, and many larger companies offer employee assistance programmes that can support you.

Plan a phased return

  • Ask for shorter days or fewer days for the first two to four weeks.
  • Agree on clear, realistic priorities with your manager.
  • Keep aftercare appointments in your calendar like any other meeting.
  • Build in a lunch break away from your desk every day.

Protecting your recovery at work

Notice the moments that might be hard: tight deadlines, travel, client dinners or celebrations. Plan ahead. Have a drink you enjoy that is alcohol-free, arrive with a reason to leave early and keep a supportive contact on speed dial. It is completely acceptable to say, “I am not drinking at the moment,” without further explanation.

Your recovery is the foundation that makes good work possible. Protect it first.

If work itself was the problem

Sometimes a job is part of what kept the cycle going: very long hours, a drinking culture or constant pressure. If so, it may be worth considering a change of role, team or career over time. Our vocational counsellors can help with CVs, interview practice and thinking through options without rushing.

Key points

  • A phased return protects both your recovery and your performance.
  • You choose how much to share, and medical details stay private.
  • Plan for stress points such as deadlines, travel and office parties.
