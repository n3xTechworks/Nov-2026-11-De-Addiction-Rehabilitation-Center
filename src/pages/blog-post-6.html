Why sleep is so disrupted

Alcohol and many drugs change the brain's sleep systems. Alcohol may help people fall asleep, but it breaks up deep and dream sleep later in the night. When use stops, the brain needs time to recalibrate, so insomnia, vivid dreams and waking at night are very common in the first weeks and can continue for a few months. This is a normal part of healing.

Poor sleep matters because tiredness makes cravings, low mood and irritability worse. That is why we treat sleep as a core part of recovery care.

Habits that help

  • Wake at the same time every day, even after a bad night. This is the single most powerful habit.
  • Get daylight within an hour of waking, ideally with a short walk.
  • Keep caffeine before noon and avoid heavy meals late in the evening.
  • Wind down for 30 to 60 minutes: dim lights, a warm shower, light reading.
  • Keep phones out of the bedroom, or at least out of reach.

If you cannot sleep

Lying in bed worrying about sleep tends to make it worse. If you have been awake for about 20 minutes, get up, go somewhere dim and do something calm until you feel sleepy, then return to bed. Breathing slowly, with a longer out-breath than in-breath, can also settle the body.

One bad night is not a failure. Sleep comes back gradually, a little more each week.

CBT for insomnia

Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the recommended first treatment for long-term sleep problems. It uses sleep scheduling, relaxation and gentle changes to unhelpful thoughts about sleep. It works well for people in recovery and avoids the risks of dependence that come with some sleeping pills.

A word on sleeping pills

Some sleeping tablets, such as benzodiazepines and “Z-drugs”, can themselves lead to dependence. Please do not stop or start them without medical advice. Our doctors can plan a gradual, comfortable taper where needed and suggest safer options.

Key points

  • Sleep problems are expected in early recovery and usually improve.
  • A steady wake-up time matters more than an early bedtime.
  • CBT for insomnia works better long term than sleeping pills.
