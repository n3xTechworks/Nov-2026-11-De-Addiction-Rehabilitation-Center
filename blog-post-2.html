Nutrition in recovery

How alcohol and drugs affect nutrients, and simple Indian meals that help body and mood heal.

Karan MehtaClinical Nutritionist
22 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Why food matters in recovery

Long-term alcohol or drug use often pushes food aside. Meals are skipped, appetite comes and goes, and the body struggles to absorb nutrients. Alcohol in particular depletes thiamine (vitamin B1), folate, magnesium and zinc. Stimulants suppress appetite, while opioids slow digestion. The result is a body that is running on empty just when it needs energy to heal.

Good nutrition will not cure addiction on its own, but it makes everything else easier: sleep improves, energy returns, mood becomes steadier and the brain has the building blocks it needs to repair.

Steady blood sugar, steadier cravings

Many people in early recovery notice strong sugar cravings. This is common and understandable, because sugar also activates the reward system. Swings in blood sugar can feel a lot like anxiety or cravings, so eating every three to four hours is one of the simplest tools we teach.

  • Pair carbohydrates with protein: idli with sambar, roti with dal, poha with peanuts.
  • Choose whole grains such as brown rice, millets and whole wheat where you can.
  • Keep a snack nearby: a banana, roasted chana or a handful of nuts.

Building a recovery plate

At Harborlight we use a simple plate guide rather than strict rules. Half the plate is vegetables or salad, a quarter is protein such as dal, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken or sprouts, and a quarter is a whole grain. Add a spoon of curd for gut health and a piece of fruit for dessert.

We are not aiming for a perfect diet. We are aiming for three regular meals that leave you feeling cared for.

Fluids and caffeine

Dehydration is common in early recovery and can cause headaches and tiredness that are easy to mistake for withdrawal. Aim for water, buttermilk, coconut water or lemon water through the day. Many people lean on tea, coffee or energy drinks instead. You do not need to give these up, but keep caffeine to the morning so it does not disturb sleep.

Do I need supplements?

Some people do, especially after heavy alcohol use, where thiamine is usually prescribed during detox to protect the brain. Your doctor may check vitamin B12, vitamin D, iron and liver function and suggest supplements based on the results. Please do not start high-dose supplements on your own; some can interact with medicines.

If you have diabetes, liver disease or an eating disorder, ask our team for a personal plan.

Key points

  • Regular meals steady blood sugar, which helps mood and cravings.
  • Protein, B vitamins and magnesium are often low in early recovery.
  • Small, consistent changes matter more than a perfect diet.
